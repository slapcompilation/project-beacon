Introduction
To do many of the tasks in this section you will need to have access to the Data Governance Officer role in Foundry - contact your administrator to be added to the group.
Markings are security controls used in Foundry to restrict access to resources (such as files, folders, datasets, or projects) based on sensitivity or organizational policy. A Marking acts as a label (e.g., "PII", "Confidential", "Case-12345") that enforces who can access the marked resource. Users must be granted the corresponding Marking to access content protected by it.
Markings travel with the data, not just with the file location. Applying or removing a Marking has immediate and broad effects due to inheritance. Users must have both the Marking and the necessary permissions to access a resource.
Markings can be applied through two methods:
1. Direct Application: 1. Markings can be directly applied to resources such as files, folders, or projects. This immediately restricts access to the marked resource and everything it contains. Direct application is a sensitive action as it affects all downstream resources, potentially limiting access for other users.
2. Marking Inheritance: This occurs in two ways: 1. Hierarchical Inheritance: When a Marking is applied to a folder or project, all contents (files and subfolders) inherit that Marking. Restricting access at a higher level automatically restricts everything nested within it. In the Foundry UI, inherited Markings are often indicated by a special icon in the resource panel. 2. Data Dependency Inheritance: If a dataset is derived from a marked dataset, the new dataset inherits the Marking. This ensures continued protection of sensitive data, even as it's transformed or used in downstream analyses. Removing a Marking from a source can potentially remove it from all downstream dependencies, unless explicitly overridden.
This dual approach to Marking application ensures comprehensive and consistent data protection across your information ecosystem.
